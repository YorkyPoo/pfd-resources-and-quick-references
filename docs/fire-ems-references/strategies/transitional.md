# Transitional Strategy

## 💡 Core Idea
- A **transitional (combination) strategy** applies exterior cooling streams from a safe location to rapidly knock down fire conditions before committing crews to an interior attack. It bridges the gap between offensive and defensive postures.

## Explanation
- **Used when:**
    - Exterior cooling will improve conditions.
    - Fire conditions require rapid reduction prior to interior attack.
    - Conditions support transition to offensive operations.
- **Sequence:** Cool from a safe location (SLICE-RS) to reduce heat and improve tenability, re-assess, then transition to an interior (offensive) attack once conditions permit.
- **Risk discipline:** Keeps crews out of an untenable flow path while still making the fire area defensible for a rescue-capable offensive push.

## Examples
- **Residential fire, vent-limited:** Heavy smoke and high heat with no safe path to the seat of the fire. Crew applies an exterior line through the window to cool, then re-enters for interior attack and primary search.
- **Short stretch to an interior door:** Quick exterior knock-down before advancing interior, buying time for the rescue profile to be confirmed.
