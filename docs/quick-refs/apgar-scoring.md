# APGAR Scoring

A **fast, retrospective summary of how well a newborn tolerated birth and transition.** It is
recorded by the team after care has started.

!!! warning "APGAR never decides whether to resuscitate"
    Resuscitation begins on **tone, breathing and heart rate**, not on a score, and never
    waits on a clock. Scoring does not delay positive-pressure ventilation or chest
    compressions. Follow your neonatal/resuscitation protocol and the OMD's direction.

## The five signs, 0 to 2 each — total 0 to 10

| Sign | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A**ppearance (colour) | Blue/pale all over | Body pink, extremities blue | Completely pink |
| **P**ulse (heart rate) | Absent | Under 100 bpm | 100 bpm or more |
| **G**rimace (reflex response) | No response to stimulation | Grimace/weak cry | Vigorous cry, cough, sneeze |
| **A**ctivity (muscle tone) | Flaccid, no movement | Some flexion, limp | Active, well-flexed motion |
| **R**espiration (breathing) | Absent | Slow, weak, irregular cry | Strong cry, good respiration |

## When it is recorded

Conventionally at **1 minute and 5 minutes** after birth. A low 1-minute score describes how
much transition help the baby needed; the **5-minute score carries more weight** about how
they are doing now. Where a 5-minute score is low, practice is to keep scoring at further
intervals as the team directs — record the times with each number rather than assuming one
figure.

## What it does and does not tell you

- It is **not** a diagnosis, not a predictor of long-term outcome, and not a measure of
  asphyxia on its own.
- Prematurity, maternal sedation, congenital anomaly, infection and successful resuscitation
  all move the number without telling you why.
- What matters on your handoff: **what you found, what you did, when, and how the baby
  responded.**
