# Poquoson Fire and Rescue Patient Care Protocols
## Cardiac - Aortic Dissection & Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm

**OVERVIEW:**

Aortic Aneurysms (AA) are a degenerative and progressively slow process where the walls of the aorta weaken and expand due to the systemic pressures of the circulatory system. The formation of aneurysms can be attributed to atherosclerosis, infection, trauma, hypertension, and inherited disorders. AAs generally form in the abdominal section of the aorta and present with weak or absent pulses in the lower extremities, cooler temperatures in the lower extremities, a central abdominal mass that can sometimes have pulsations, and abdominal and/or back pain. If the aneurysm ruptures, chance of survival is very low and requires immediate surgical intervention.

Aortic Dissections usually occur in the thoracic cavity when the aortic intima is torn away, exposing the media layer. The pulse pressure from the systemic circulation then begins to dissect the two layers of the aortic wall and creates a false lumen or pouch in the wall of the aorta. Conditions associated with the formation of an aortic dissection include: hypertension, Marfan's Syndrome, aortic valve abnormalities, immune disorders, atherosclerosis, and patients in the third trimester of pregnancy. When left untreated, about 33% of patients die within the first 24 hours, and 50% die within 48 hours. The 2-week mortality rate approaches 75% in patients with undiagnosed ascending aortic dissection.

### Assessment & Criteria

=== "History of Present Illness"
    - Age
    - Medications (especially Viagra, Levitra, Cialis)
    - PMH (MI, Angina, DM, HTN)
    - Allergies (ASA, Morphine)
    - Onset
    - Quality (crushing, sharp, dull, constant, etc.)
    - Region / Radiation / Referred
    - Severity (1-10)
    - Time (duration / repetition)
    - Previous cardiac history
    - Risk factors: hypertension, diabetes, smoking, family history

=== "Signs & Symptoms"
    - Weak / absent pulses in lower extremities
    - Cooler temperatures in lower extremities
    - Central abdominal mass with possible pulsations
    - Anterior chest / upper back pain
    - "Tearing" sensation in back or chest
    - Tachycardia
    - Hypertension (may be present)
    - Hypotension (if rupture occurs)
    - Dyspnea
    - Diaphoresis
    - Syncope or near-syncope
    - Anxiety, sense of impending doom
    - Murmur of aortic insufficiency (on physical exam)

=== "Differential Considerations"
    - **Acute Myocardial Infarction (STEMI/NSTEMI)**
    - **Angina vs. MI**
    - **Pericarditis**
    - **Pulmonary Embolism**
    - **Asthma / COPD**
    - **Pneumothorax**
    - **GI reflux, hiatal hernia**
    - **Esophageal spasm**
    - **Chest wall injury or pain**
    - **Pleural pain**
    - **Trauma vs. Medical**

### Pearls

1. **Treatment Goals:** The primary treatment goals are to maintain systolic BP 90-120 mmHg and heart rate between 50-80 bpm. Avoid aggressive fluid resuscitation in normotensive or hypertensive patients — this may exacerbate the dissection.

2. **Do Not Delay Transport:** Do not delay transport for any reason if possible; interventions should be done enroute to appropriate facility. Time to definitive surgical care is the critical factor in survival.

3. **Abdominal Mass Detection:** Abdominal mass may not be palpable in obese patients. Maintain high index of suspicion based on history and presentation.

4. **Physical Examination:** Physical examination may reveal a murmur of aortic insufficiency. Check bilateral blood pressures — a difference of >20 mmHg between arms is suggestive of aortic dissection.

5. **Type A vs. Type B Dissection:** Type A dissection occurs in the ascending aorta and is a surgical emergency with extremely high mortality if untreated. Type B dissection occurs just distal to the left subclavian artery and may be managed medically in some cases.

6. **Avoid Aspirin in Suspected Dissection:** DO NOT administer [Aspirin](Aspirin.md) if aortic dissection or AAA is suspected. Antiplatelet therapy can worsen bleeding and complicate surgical repair.

7. **Fluid Administration Caution:** When administering fluids to maintain SBP > 90 mmHg, use caution in patients with known or suspected congestive heart failure or decreased ejection fraction. Large volumes of rapidly administered fluids may lead to pulmonary edema and respiratory distress. Administer smaller boluses (250 mL then reassess).

8. **Vasopressor Administration:** Administer vasopressors through the largest, most proximal IV site available (AC or higher preferable) or IO. Verify patency prior to initiating infusion and monitor closely for signs of extravasation.

9. **Avoid Positive Pressure if Hypotensive:** Use caution when considering positive pressure ventilation for patients with hypotension, particularly due to intravascular hypovolemia (reduced pre-load). Intubation and positive pressure ventilation may lead to a sudden drop in blood pressure or circulatory collapse/cardiac arrest.

10. **Associated Conditions:** Conditions associated with aortic dissection include: hypertension, Marfan's Syndrome, aortic valve abnormalities, immune disorders, atherosclerosis, and patients in the third trimester of pregnancy.

11. Refer to Death & Criteria for Withholding Resuscitation Poquoson Protocol if patient decompensates to cardiac arrest.

12. Associated factors affecting hemodynamics (trauma, intoxication, illness) must be considered when determining medication administration.

13. If law-enforcement restraints utilized, officer should accompany patient; consider exchanging for EMS-sanctioned restraints.

14. Patients in law enforcement custody must have officer present at all times.
