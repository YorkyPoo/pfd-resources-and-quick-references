# Poquoson Fire and Rescue Patient Care Protocols
## Procedures - Spinal Motion Restriction (SMR)

### Overview
Spinal Motion Restriction (SMR) is utilized to minimize movement of the spine in trauma patients meeting specific criteria.

### Indications
- Non-penetrating trauma AND:
    - Altered LOC (GCS <15)
    - Midline neck/back pain/tenderness
    - Focal neurologic deficits
    - Anatomic deformity of spine
    - Distracting circumstances/injury
    - High-risk MOI (pediatrics)

### Procedure
| # | Action / Intervention | EMR | EMT | A | I | P |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Maintain manual C-spine stabilization while applying collar. | • | • | • | • | • |
| 2 | Perform focused neurologic exam (GCS, motor, sensory). | • | • | • | • | • |
| 3 | Utilize backboard/scoop only as a lift/move device. Once on stretcher, remove backboard/scoop. | • | • | • | • | • |
| 4 | If head elevation is required, elevate the head while maintaining spinal alignment. | • | • | • | • | • |

### Pearls
*   Long spine boards are extrication devices, not for prolonged immobilization on the stretcher.
*   Ambulatory patients meeting SMR criteria may be assisted into a supine position while minimizing spinal movement.
*   Pregnant patients (2nd/3rd trimester) should be transported with backboard angled/turned slightly to the left.
*   If unsure, contact Medical Control.
*   Utilize padding/shoulder rolls for pediatrics to prevent neck flexion.
