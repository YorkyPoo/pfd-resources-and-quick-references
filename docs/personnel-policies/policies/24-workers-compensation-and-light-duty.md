# Workers’ Compensation and Light Duty

_**Section No.: 24**  ·  Effective: 3_

## Purpose

It is the objective of the City of Poquoson, Virginia that all City employees receive
benefits provided by the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Act, Title 65.2 of the Code of
Virginia (1950), as amended upon suffering a work-related injury, illness (occupational
disease), or death. The purpose of the Workers’ Compensation Acct is to provide financial
benefits and medical care. The Finance Department is responsible for administering the
Workers’ Compensation program and shall provide the expertise and assistance to both
the management and employees.

## Scope

This policy shall apply to all full-time and_ part-time employees and shall provide the
statutory benefits required under the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Act.

|. Medical Expenses: Medical costs for conditions caused by a compensable
job-related injury or disease, subject to a specific period or for as long as
necessary if the employee files a claim with the Virginia Workers’
Compensation Commission within the required time period.

2. Temporary Total Disability Payments: Wage replacement while

temporarily unable to work due to a compensable job-related injury or disease,
subject to a maximum period.

3. Temporary Partial Disability Payments: Partial wage replacement after

returning to work in a modified, light duty, or part-time job at a decreased
wage, subject to a maximum period.

4. Permanent Partial Impairment Payments: Compensation for a specific

number of hours for permanent loss or loss of use of a body part as the result
of a compensable job-related injury. Vision, hearing loss and disfigurement
may also be compensated.

5. Permanent Total Disability Payments: Wage replacement for life; only

payable under specific circumstances; total loss of both hands, arms, feet,
legs, eyes, or any two in the same compensable accident, or compensable job-
related incidents resulting in permanent paralysis or severe brain injury.

6. Death Benefits: A specific sum payable for a specified period to qualifying
dependents of an employee who dies as the result of a compensable job-
related injury or disease.

## Definitions
Injury

An injury is defined as an injury by accident arising out of and in the course of
employment.

Occupational Disease

An occupational disease is defined as a disease arising out of and in the course of
employment, but, unless otherwise provided by the Virginia Workers’ Compensation
Act, not an ordinary disease of life to which the general public is exposed outside of
employment.

Workers’ Compensation

Workers’ Compensation is defined as a type of leave from employment which results
from an employee’s incapacity to work, and which has been determined to have resulted
from an injury or occupational disease such that the employee is entitled to benefits
required by the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Act.

City Designated Panel Physicians

1. Section 65.2-603 of the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Act requires that
injured employees choose a physician from a panel selected by the employer.

2. Employer authorized panel physicians and medical treatment facilities must be
used at all times by employees receiving treatment for job-related
injury/illness.

3. Exception to employer authorized panel physicians and medical treatment
facilities may be made in the case of an emergency requiring immediate
medical treatment that occurs outside of normal working hours.

4. Failure to secure treatment from an authorized panel physician could result in a
denial of benefits.

5. Any change in treating physician requires approval of the individual insurance
carrier prior to treatment.

Second Opinions

Employees receiving medical treatment for a compensable injury or illness may request a
second medical opinion or a change in treating physician by contacting the individual
insurance carrier and requesting prior approval.

Use of Leave to Supplement Workers’ Compensation Benefit

Employees may supplement the Workers’ Compensation benefit by one-third (1/3) using
accmied annual, sick or compensatory leave. Employees may elect to continue to receive
their salary until such time as all leave has been exhausted and then they must accept
payment from Workers’ Compensation. Checks received from Workers’ Compensation
while receiving full salary from the City must be turned over to the City. Leave will be
reinstated to the employee based on the amount of the check divided by the hourly rate of
the employee.

Return to Work

1. An injured employee must return to work as soon as he/she is released for
regular, restrictive, or light duty by the treating physician.

2. A Functional Capacities form must be completed by the treating physician
and submitted to the Personnel Officer prior to the employee’s return to
regular, restrictive or light duty.

3. The employee shall not be permitted to perform light duty unless approved by
the attending physician.

4. Employees on light duty assignment may return to their regular duties upon
receipt of a Functional Capacities Form signed by the employee’s attending
physician attesting to the employee’s physical and mental fitness to
resume regular duties.

Line of Duty Act (LODA)

It shall be the responsibility of the Police Chief and Fire Chief, respectively to train their
personnel, including volunteers and auxiliary police, on the Line of Duty Act (LODA).

Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA)

1. Injuries or illnesses approved by Workers’ Compensation generally meet the
eligibility for FMLA.

2. An absence due to Workers’ Compensation will be charged to the employee’s
annual 12-week entitlement to FMLA benefits, providing the employee
meets the definition of eligibility as listed in the current version of
Regulation 4835, Family Medical Leave.

One-Year Limitation

1, Employees who have not returned to work within one calendar year after a

compensable work-related illness or injury shall be placed on leave without
pay status or terminated.

. Employees may apply for disability retirement if they meet the eligibility
requirements established by the Virginia Retirement System.

Claim Denials

1. Leave Time

a. Ifa claim is denied, the employee shall be charged sick leave for
all work time for which the employee was absent.

. Compensatory leave, annual leave, or leave without pay shall be used
if sick leave is exhausted.

If the employce has no accumulated leave, the Personnel Officer shall

make arrangements with the employee to repay any compensation
advanced.

2. Letter of Denial

a. If an employee receives a letter of denial from the insurance carrier,
there are two courses of action that may be pursued:

(1) Forward the letter along with the proper forms to the employee’s
health insurance carrier.

(2) File a request for a hearing with the Industrial Commission of

Virginia. The Commission’s address will be provided in the letter
of denial.

Workers’ Compensation Appeal

1. An employee has the right to appeal denial of Workers’ Compensation
Coverage to the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission.

2. Forms for this purpose are available in the Finance Department.

Limitations and Exclusions

1. Workers’ Compensation benefits do not cover injuries incurred while
traveling to and from work or during activities not required by the
employee’s position or work assignment.

2. Not all injuries that occur during work hours are compensable under the
definitions provided by the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission.

3. An employee receiving Workers’ Compensation wages is prohibited from
engaging in activities that may impair his or her recovery, such as
strenuous recreational or other physical activities, including vacations,
without approval of the authorized treating physician and notification to
the third-party administrator.

4. Failure to report such activities may affect benefits from Workers’
Compensation.

Light Duty Assigninent

1. Whenever an injured or ill employee is temporarily unable to return to his/her
regular duty, but is considered medically fit to perform less strenuous tasks,
efforts shall be made by the Personnel Officer in consultation with the

employee’s Department Head or designee to find a light duty assignment for
that employee.

2. Such an assignment may not necessarily be in the employee’s regular
department and shall not exceed 90 consecutive days. However, 90 days may
extended to 180 days by the City Manager on an individual case by case basis.

3. Fitness for light duty assignment shall be coordinated with the treating
physician and shall be such that it accommodates identified restrictions.

4. During the period of light duty assignment, no employee shall be
permitted to work more than their normal working hours.

5. A light duty assignment shall not be made in any case where there is no
possibility of full recovery. Proper medical certification shall be utilized
in arriving at this decision.

6. Fire Department employees who are placed on light duty shall be converted to
a 40-hour per week work schedule. Accrual of leave during this period will
be based on the 40-hour schedule. Once the employee returns to active duty
in the Fire Department the normal leave accrual for that Department will be
restored.

. While on light duty, Police Department employees will be assigned to work a

40-hour week, Monday through Friday, from 8:30 a.m. until 4:30 p.m.
Department members on this schedule will accrue annual leave and sick leave
in accordance with the applicable policies in the City Personnel Policy
Manual which govern employees working such a schedule. Police
Department employees on light duty status are limited to 40 hours of credited
work time per week. Employees on light duty status shall not be approved to
work additional hours that would require the City to compensate them with
overtime pay or compensatory time unless the additional hours are pre-
approved by the City Manager. Once the employee is cleared to return to
active duty, the employee will be assigned to an approved work schedule that
is appropriate for his/her position.
