# Contributing to the PFD Resources & Quick References site

Thanks for helping build the playbook that didn't exist when we were rookies.

## What belongs here
- **Craft knowledge** that isn't in textbooks: local water supply logic, first-due decision sequences, station practice, study references tied to **National Registry (NREMT) standards**.
- Reference material you have the **right to share**: your own notes, public-domain/government sources (NFPA public summaries, OSHA, county GIS with permission), or licensed material.

## What never belongs here
- **Anything patient-related.** No call narratives, no patient identifiers, no case photos — not even anonymized. Zero exceptions; this is a hard gate.
- **Proprietary material you don't have the right to redistribute** — subscription question banks, paid textbook chapters, copyrighted exam content. (We reference textbooks; we don't repost them.)
- **Another department's internal SOPs** without their explicit approval.

## How to propose content (today)
1. Open a **GitHub issue** titled `Proposal: <topic>` and paste your content, or
2. Fork → edit the markdown under `docs/` → open a **pull request**.

## Format rules (keep the site consistent)
- One topic per page; H2 sections; tables must stay readable at 390px phone width (wrap wide tables in the existing `.fx`/`.dz` components — see `docs/pediatric-dosing/dosing-tables.md` for the pattern).
- Scope matrices: blank means NOT authorized; `MC` (Medical Control) must be explicit per tier — never implied or inherited upward.
- Cite the source of every protocol claim (doc name, standard, edition).

## Where this is going
Submissions will be triaged by an AI pre-filter (accuracy, scope, format, privacy) with final approval always by a human editor. If you're contributing: write for the firefighter on the tailboard at 0300, not for the exam.
