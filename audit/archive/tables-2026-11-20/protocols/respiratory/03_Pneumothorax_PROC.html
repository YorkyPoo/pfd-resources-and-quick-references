# Level Action / Intervention
1 EMR Universal Patient Care / Scene Safety:
• Ensure scene safety and appropriate PPE.
• Bring all necessary equipment to patient side.
2 EMR Primary Assessment:
• Assess airway, breathing, circulation, and disability.
• Assess vital signs and SpO2.
• Identify life threats immediately.
3 EMT Oxygen Administration:
• Administer Oxygen via nasal cannula or mask as needed to maintain SpO2 > 94%.
• Titrate to patient condition.
4 EMR Positioning:
• Position patient for comfort (typically supine or semi-Fowler's).
4a A • Establish IV/IO access.
4b I • Continuous cardiac and pulse oximetry monitoring.
6 I Needle Decompression - Site Selection:
• Locate 2nd intercostal space in mid-clavicular line on affected side.
• Alternate site: 4th intercostal space, mid-axillary line.
7 I Needle Decompression - Equipment:
• Attach 10 mL 0.9% Sodium Chloride syringe to 14-gauge catheter needle.
• Use longest available needle (3.25 inch preferred for adult/trauma patients).
8 I Needle Decompression - Procedure:
• Aspirate syringe while advancing over the superior aspect of the rib.
• Insert perpendicular to the skin into the pleural space.
• Continue until "pop" felt or air/blood/fluid freely aspirated.
• ⚠ Do NOT advance needle further.
• Advance catheter over needle into pleural space.
• Remove needle, leave catheter in place.
9 I Bilateral Chest Decompression (Traumatic Cardiac Arrest):
• For traumatic cardiac arrest with suspicion of thoracoabdominal trauma, perform bilateral needle decompression.
10 I Reassessment After Decompression:
• Continuously reassess vital signs, respiratory effort, and compliance.
• Auscultate for return of breath sounds.
• Monitor for hemodynamic improvement.
11 I Repeat Decompression if Needed:
• Consider repeating procedure for continued or recurrent hemodynamic instability or absent breath sounds.
12 A Hemodynamic Support:
• 0.9% Sodium Chloride fluid bolus if hypotensive (SBP < 90 mmHg or MAP < 65).
• Titrate fluids to perfusion.
12a I • Consider vasopressor support if cardiogenic shock present.
13 I Advanced Airway Management (if indicated):
• Prepare for or perform endotracheal intubation if respiratory failure. Patients 12 years and older.
• ⚠ Positive pressure ventilation (BVM/CPAP) may worsen or induce pneumothorax.
13a P • Orally intubate. Patients < 12 years old.
13b P • If unable to intubate and unable to oxygenate, proceed immediately to Surgical Airway.
14 EMT Transport Considerations:
• Transport to appropriate trauma center if traumatic etiology.
• Alert receiving facility of needle decompression performed.
• Consider air medical transport for severe cases.
15 EMR Close Monitoring En Route:
• Continuous reassessment en route.
15a I • Monitor for recurrent tension pneumothorax.
• Reassess respiratory effort and compliance after decompression.
